The AFTER side of [the old world](2026-07-28-the-0.13-old-world.md),
one phase at a time as the batch lands. Same instruments, same

Provenance: branch `0.13.0`, workspace 0.13.0, release build, same
machine and artifact as the old-world file.

## Phase: wreck decay and the retarget radius

The coupled pair, landed as one bless:

- `WRECK_DECAY_TICKS` 40 -> 300. The old cadence rotted 87-93% of
  battlefield salvage before anyone could reach it (the old-world
  probes measured 1-3% recovery); at 15 s per salvage a Scuttler
  wreck lasts 4.5 min, a Sentinel 10, a Bombard 22.5 — a deliberate
  trip on a vast map, still never a bank.
- `RETARGET_RADIUS` 10 -> 2. The measured maximum intra-deposit
  Chebyshev diameter across all 25 shipped scenarios is 2; the old
  radius reached whole other deposits, chained hop to hop (max
  measured drift 51 tiles, 60% of hops toward a hostile Foundry),
  and 12% of hops targeted tiles the owner had never seen. The jump
  histogram was bimodal at exactly this cut: hops were 1-2 tiles
  (finishing the deposit) or >=4 (marching). Now the harvester
  finishes its deposit, delivers, and goes idle — the badge flow.
- Wreck tiles are no longer `replacement_node` candidates. This is
  the guard that makes slow decay safe: without it every battlefield
  becomes a permanent auto-retarget magnet and item one recreates
  the chain-walk on a field that never expires. Battlefield salvage
  is directed work now — explicit Harvest only, both for the human
  and for the bots' economy channels (which always could).
- `dead_nodes` hygiene in both bot minds: the audit blacklists a
  node only if it still REPORTS value at audit time. An honestly
  drained source reads as empty and needs no entry — blacklisting it
  (the old behavior) would poison the tile against every future
  wreck landing there, and with harvesters now going idle routinely
  the poisoning stopped being rare.

| wins /240 | 152 | 143 | 138 | 130 |

More faithful policy-following measured WORSE — the policy trained
under the old economy, so fidelity to it is fidelity to a
miscalibration; Medium's extra blunder noise was accidentally
breaking the pattern. The same patience artifact 0.10 recorded.

## Phase: displaced bodies walk off the foundation, and the whole crew builds

The stacked-build fix. Friendly machines no longer block placement:
the founder builds under its own feet and steps to the canonical
doorstep, and every other friendly on the footprint — allies
included — deals deterministically onto the perimeter ring as the
site claims the ground. A visible HOSTILE machine still denies its
tile, and all relocation runs strictly after the last rejection path,
so a refused command leaves no trace on the hash.

Exactly 5 fixture entries moved; zero golden PNG bytes. The gates did
not blink: ladder 48/53/63/70 with strict pace, scripted rungs and

on the parent, the 500-unit brawl 7,083 vs 6,943 — the walk-off is
free.

## Phase: buildings stop blocking bullets

fire; buildings — own, allied, hostile — no longer do. The old-world
siege probe predicted the flip and it reproduced through the shipped
instrument, at the one pitch where it was ever real:

| pitch 1 (dense garrison) | A sentinel:12 (1080) | B turret:6 (600) |
|---|---|---|
| old rule | **360** (both legs wipe B, 673/855 ticks) | 0 |
| terrain-only | 0 | **250** (both legs wipe A, 649/644 ticks) |

At the shipped pitch 3 the probe is byte-identical before and after —
sparse geometry never put a turret on a fire line, and neither does
tick-2000 bot play: the hash fixtures passed WITHOUT blessing. Three
pin tests fail 3/3 under the stashed old rule and pass under the new
one. A fortification now defends at full strength however densely it
is packed; cover is terrain's job alone.

## Phase: the build ghost reaches remembered ground

Fog placement Part A, the deferred founding: the shell may emit a
build order onto remembered-but-unseen ground, and the founder pays
on ARRIVAL, when the ground is seen and `can_place` can judge it
honestly. Bots keep today's predicate (Part B is the campaign's).

The headline property: **hash-inert, proven** — fixtures and goldens
byte-identical unblessed, because no shipped command stream contains
a deferred emission yet. Seven sim tests pin the contract (a rejected
defer leaves no hash trace; a broke founder stalls on arrival; Stop
spends nothing) plus a two-worlds property test over every anchor:
the hidden-enemy verdict must match between a world that saw and a
world that remembered. That test earned its keep in development — its
first formulation caught idle enemies aggroing into visible range,
and was restructured so the squad creeps onto remembered ground only
after sight is lost.

## Phase: harvesters weld machines

`Command::RepairUnit` — the harvester's welding torch turns on
machines, billed through the same ceiling-prepaid milli-scrap meter
as building repair, plus the Phase 1 carry-forward: the meter clamp
that refunds a welder whose whole step the hp ceiling rejected.
Hash-inert, proven unblessed (no bot emits it — a v6 verb). The
billing pins: stacked welders on a 1-hp wound spend exactly 1 scrap
between them; the first coin of any job is prepaid.

## Phase: the Repair Bay

`BuildingKind::RepairBay` — a paid welding ring, pulled into 0.13
core so the campaign trains with it in the roster. Dials as shipped:
cost 200, radius 4.0, 1 hp per 8 ticks per patient, 850 permille
billing. That rate is 2.5 hp/s — about a quarter of one Turret's
damage rate, so focused fire always out-paces the ring.

The turtle-buff risk was the tuning question, answered through the
matchup instrument:

| probe | shape | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| R2 | bay behind turrets, price-matched vs plain army | attackers win 315/100 — the bay does NOT out-defend its price statically |
| R3 | mirror armies, one side + bay | hp-weighted 260 vs 60 — a 200-scrap bay roughly pays for itself sustaining an equal army inside its ring |
| R4 | same 400 garrison scrap as turret:4 | turrets defend far better — no turtle superiority |
| R5 | repairbay:2 | identical to one bay — no runaway stacking |
| R6 | attacker priced up by the bay's cost | attackers win 495/200 — the bay loses to its own price in attackers |

State hashes byte-identical (the bot cannot build it until gym v6);
the only golden that moved was the showcase render, which now shows
the ring.

## The parity-alternate movement::run experiment, run and retired

The standing candidate engine experiment (Known Issues carried it as
"hash-moving; needs a bless and a re-measure"): alternate
`movement::run`'s unit iteration by tick parity like the brain loop,
aimed at the near-deterministic mirror residual.

Implemented and measured. The premise was wrong — the change is
provably inert. Path following has no cross-unit reads: each body
consults only its own path, the map, and the (frozen-during-movement)
building list, and writes only itself and its own travel slot, so
iteration order cannot reach the outcome. The empirical side agreed
everywhere:

| probe | old order | parity-alternated |
|---|---|---|
| 25-scenario hash fixtures (tick 2k) | green | green UNBLESSED |
| skirmish `run --bots`, 120k ticks | 0x2e7bc33a6613a1e3 | 0x2e7bc33a6613a1e3 |
| `sweep` skirmish Medium, 48 games | 48/48 decided, seats 9-39, median 5614 | identical output |
| `duel` expert/expert, 12 games, 120k cap | 6-6, 0 draws, median 8056 | identical output |
| movement_lab, 9 scenarios | green | green |

A bit-identical sim cannot improve mirrors, so the keep-condition was
unreachable and the patch came out cleanly — no bless, no gate
re-measure needed. The real finding: the id-order coupling in
movement lives in `resolve_collisions`, whose pass direction has
ALREADY alternated by tick parity since 0.6. What stays fixed is
pair-visit order within a relaxation pass; a future candidate aims
there, and that one genuinely moves hashes.
