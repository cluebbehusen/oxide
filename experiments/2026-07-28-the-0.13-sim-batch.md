# The 0.13 sim batch (2026-07-28)

The AFTER side of [the old world](2026-07-28-the-0.13-old-world.md),
one phase at a time as the batch lands. Same instruments, same
invocations; read the columns side by side.

Provenance: branch `0.13.0`, workspace 0.13.0, release build, same
machine and artifact as the old-world file.

## Phase: wreck decay and the retarget radius

The coupled pair, landed as one bless:

- `WRECK_DECAY_TICKS` 40 -> 300. The old cadence rotted 87-93% of
  battlefield salvage before anyone could reach it (the old-world
  probes measured 1-3% recovery); at 15 s per salvage a Scuttler
  wreck lasts 4.5 min, a Sentinel 10, a Bombard 22.5 — a deliberate
  trip on a vast map, still never a bank.
- `RETARGET_RADIUS` 10 -> 2. The measured maximum intra-deposit
  Chebyshev diameter across all 25 shipped scenarios is 2; the old
  radius reached whole other deposits, chained hop to hop (max
  measured drift 51 tiles, 60% of hops toward a hostile Foundry),
  and 12% of hops targeted tiles the owner had never seen. The jump
  histogram was bimodal at exactly this cut: hops were 1-2 tiles
  (finishing the deposit) or >=4 (marching). Now the harvester
  finishes its deposit, delivers, and goes idle — the badge flow.
- Wreck tiles are no longer `replacement_node` candidates. This is
  the guard that makes slow decay safe: without it every battlefield
  becomes a permanent auto-retarget magnet and item one recreates
  the chain-walk on a field that never expires. Battlefield salvage
  is directed work now — explicit Harvest only, both for the human
  and for the bots' economy channels (which always could).
- `dead_nodes` hygiene in both bot minds: the audit blacklists a
  node only if it still REPORTS value at audit time. An honestly
  drained source reads as empty and needs no entry — blacklisting it
  (the old behavior) would poison the tile against every future
  wreck landing there, and with harvesters now going idle routinely
  the poisoning stopped being rare.

## The ladder inversion, measured

The in-tree 80-match tripwire went red on the first run: the middle
rungs swapped.

| slate | Easy | Medium | Hard | Expert |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| old world (80) | 39 · 2.038M | 56 · 1.330M | 56 · 1.270M | 60 · 1.067M |
| new economy, old dials (80) | 48 · 1.819M | 53 · 1.445M | 48 · 1.539M | 70 · 0.768M |

Medium and Hard already TIED on wins in the old world; the gate held
on a 4.5% pace margin. The economy re-roll flipped it, and the
widened instrument (`yardstick --seeds 30`, 240 matches/rung) proved
it was no slate artifact: Easy 118, Medium 162, **Hard 143**,
Expert 210.

Why: the retarget change makes idle harvesters routine, and an idle
harvester waits one think for its next job — the cadence dial became
the economy's re-tasking latency. The skill knob could not buy it
back; at fixed cadence 24 it read NON-monotone on the widened slate:

| skill @ cadence 24 | 750 | 850 (shipped) | 900 | 950 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| wins /240 | 152 | 143 | 138 | 130 |

More faithful policy-following measured WORSE — the policy trained
under the old economy, so fidelity to it is fidelity to a
miscalibration; Medium's extra blunder noise was accidentally
breaking the pattern. The same patience artifact 0.10 recorded.

## The parity-alternate movement::run experiment, run and retired

The standing candidate engine experiment (Known Issues carried it as
"hash-moving; needs a bless and a re-measure"): alternate
`movement::run`'s unit iteration by tick parity like the brain loop,
aimed at the near-deterministic mirror residual.

Implemented and measured. The premise was wrong — the change is
provably inert. Path following has no cross-unit reads: each body
consults only its own path, the map, and the (frozen-during-movement)
building list, and writes only itself and its own travel slot, so
iteration order cannot reach the outcome. The empirical side agreed
everywhere:

| probe | old order | parity-alternated |
|---|---|---|
| 25-scenario hash fixtures (tick 2k) | green | green UNBLESSED |
| skirmish `run --bots`, 120k ticks | 0x2e7bc33a6613a1e3 | 0x2e7bc33a6613a1e3 |
| `sweep` skirmish Medium, 48 games | 48/48 decided, seats 9-39, median 5614 | identical output |
| `duel` expert/expert, 12 games, 120k cap | 6-6, 0 draws, median 8056 | identical output |
| movement_lab, 9 scenarios | green | green |

A bit-identical sim cannot improve mirrors, so the keep-condition was
unreachable and the patch came out cleanly — no bless, no gate
re-measure needed. The real finding: the id-order coupling in
movement lives in `resolve_collisions`, whose pass direction has
ALREADY alternated by tick parity since 0.6. What stays fixed is
pair-visit order within a relaxation pass; a future candidate aims
there, and that one genuinely moves hashes.

## The roster rebalance: Buzzard 160 -> 120, Darter 90 -> 100

Decision 5 settled on balance toward par, and the old world localized
the gap to one slot: Darter over Buzzard at par cost on both value and
clock, Flakhound already ahead of both wings, Wisp/Talon at par. The
factorial's mixed-roster marginal read Ferrous 21.5% [19.5, 23.6] at
the batch's edge — byte-comparable to the old world's 21.7%, so
nothing else in the batch had touched the roster picture.

Shipped: Buzzard 160 -> 120 scrap (train 240 -> 180), Darter 90 ->
100 (train 140 -> 150). Damage, hp, speed, cadence, range: untouched.
The identities are the numbers that stayed — durable burst against
fast harass, now priced like it.

The iteration, all on seed base 7000, skirmish Medium, 24 seeds:

| candidate | mixed-roster marginal | notes |
|---|---|---|
| old prices | 21.5% [19.5, 23.6] | the before |
| buzzard 120 | 23.5% [21.4, 25.7] | the frozen-policy ceiling of a pure Ferrous buff — the policy declines to build what it trained to decline |
| + darter dmg 8 -> 7 | 24.2% [22.1, 26.4] | a dps trim the harass loop barely feels |
| + darter 110 (dmg 8) | 46.9% [44.4, 49.4] | in band, but Long Haul fell to 16/24 decided at 60k |
| + darter 100 (dmg 8) | **48.5% [46.0, 51.1]** | shipped |

The price lever works where the damage lever does not because the
frozen policy's spending is the coupling: it puts 17.4% of army value
into Darters, so taxing the unit taxes the strategy the policy
actually plays, while buffing the Buzzard subsidizes one it refuses.
FC/CF cells landed 44.7%/47.6% (from 23.2%/80.3%); the same-faction
mirrors stayed distinct (FF grinds at median 7,763 vs CC 6,394 — the
rosters are par, not mirrors). The geometry term survives untouched
either side of the bless (44.8/59.6 before, 40.3/54.5 after) — it was
never roster.

The arena at par cost against a common sentinel:16 line (1,440):
Buzzard@120 x12 keeps 1440 purchase / 1204 hp-weighted, wipes at ~287
ticks; Darter@100 x14 keeps 1250 / 1062, wipes at ~221. Buzzard wins
on retention, Darter on clock and 1.7x map speed. Both anti-air
counters still wipe both wings (Flakhound 1440/1338 over darter:14 at
~113 ticks); Wisp/Talon still flips its verdict on the seat swap.
dps-per-cost still reads 0.083 vs 0.107 — the paper ratio the arena
does not honor, hp priced in, same lesson as the Flakhound.

What the tax cost, measured. Cheap-darter tempo was ending games: the
skirmish factorial's medians moved 5.7k -> 6.8k, the decisiveness
sweep's median 5.5k -> 6.4k (still 48/48 decided, seat lean softened
9-39 -> 16-32), and the pace slate reads 373/384 decided at 60k
against the old world's 374. But the slate total hides a trade:
Continental Divide and The Crossing went to 24/24 while **Long Haul
and Oxide Flats fell to 19/24 each** — and Long Haul's old-world
24/24 at median 4,859 was seat1 winning EVERY game: its decisiveness
was the imbalance. At par the frozen policy's passivity stall (the
known all-neural artifact) surfaces there instead. Retraining-era
work; the campaign trains under these prices.

Gates: the liveness gate needed one measured re-derivation — the two
vast 1v1 maps stopped firing their first shot inside 12k ticks
(decision medians 18.6k/21.9k, march-and-buildup on 100+ tile
routes), so the vast class's horizon is now 24k while every floor
stays put. The ladder re-rolled and still orders: yardstick win
counts 38 < 55 < 56 < 70, Expert top outright and up from its
on-loan 60/80 to 70/80, zero unresolved; `neural_ladder`,
`tier_ladder`, `bot_duel`, `movement_lab` all green untouched. The
composition probe: buzzard 0.8% -> 2.7% of army value (cheapest-first
lowering converts the AirGround verb more often at 120), darter
17.4% -> 7.0%, decided 114 -> 126 of 150, per-seat entropy p10 1.54
-> 1.55. The Expert skirmish lean flipped sides (34-6 Cupric -> 35-1
Ferrous, seat-swapped pairs) — the near-deterministic residual class
whipsawing as it always does under a sim change; the shipped Medium
default is where the par claim lives.
