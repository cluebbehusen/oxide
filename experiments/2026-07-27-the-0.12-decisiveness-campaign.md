# The 0.12 decisiveness campaign (2026-07-27)

A Medium-difficulty playtest (2026-07) read as: the bot exhausted its
home scrap, never expanded, never attacked, and the game died of
boredom. The 0.12 response splits three ways — engine/UX fixes,
no-retrain bot work, and a future retraining campaign. This file is
the no-retrain bot work: the instruments, the personality clamp, and
the Medium re-meter. All win rates seat-swapped unless noted; sweeps
and duels deal personalities per seat unless noted.

## The instruments

The recorded decisiveness numbers (the 0.11 flipped-probe's 8-13/24
undecided) came from campaign scripting that never landed in-tree, so
the phases that gate on decisiveness got their instruments first:

- `driver sweep` — N seeds of bot-vs-bot on one 1v1 map at one level,
  each seed played in both personality orientations (the dealt pair
  exchanged between the seats). Decided/undecided, seat bias that
  survives the exchange, decision-tick medians.
- `driver duel` — two ladder profiles (candidate skill/cadence dials
  included) fighting seat-swapped across seeds.
- `driver yardstick` — one profile vs all four scripted tiers, N
  seeds per tier, both seats, fixed 500 personality: the widened form
  of the in-tree 24-match gate, and the doctrinal strength instrument
  (neural head-to-heads reward patience; 0.10 learned that the hard
  way).

Cold-start validation: the sweep on the shipped 0.11 configuration
(skirmish, Medium, 24 seeds x 2, 40k cap) read 33 victories / 0
draws / 15 undecided and a 2-31 seat lean surviving the personality
exchange — reproducing both the 0.11 note's undecided shape and the
known artifact-specific Cupric lean, from nothing but the new code.

## The personality clamp

Every shipped map deals `aggression: None`, uniform 0..=1000, and the
league's style reward pays a low-aggression seat for its army NOT
fighting — a dealt deep turtle IS a bot that never attacks. Measured:
in the baseline sweep the undecided games' mean min-dealt aggression
was 217 vs 422 in the decided ones.

The deal now draws 250..=900 (`bot::deal_aggression`, one definition;
balance-probe and the sweep call it instead of replicating the
stream). Explicit picks keep the full range — wizard Turtle still
means 100.

Honest result: the turtle-correlated undecided class disappeared, and
total undecided barely moved (15/48 -> 14/48). Re-dealt games find a
personality-independent stall attractor at mid aggressions (442-882).
Lesson: the clamp is the *human-facing* fix (no dealt never-attacks
opponents); symmetric-mirror stalling is a different disease. It got
its own phase, below.

## The re-meter that measurement rewrote

Hypothesis going in: Medium (skill 620, cadence 36 — 19% hesitation,
2.25x slower than the trained cadence 16) is mis-metered weak, hence
the stalling; pick stronger dials off a candidate grid.

The wide yardstick (6 seeds/tier, 48 matches) refuted it:

| profile | total | vs Prime |
|---|---|---|
| easy | 34 | 5 |
| **medium 620/c36 (shipped)** | **42** | 8 |
| hard | 46 | 10 |
| expert | 48 | 12 |
| 620/c26 | 41 | 7 |
| 700/c36 | 40 | 7 |
| 660/c28 | 37 | 5 |
| 700/c30 | 37 | 6 |

Every "stronger" candidate measured WEAKER. The skill knob is trained
*conditioning*, not a pure handicap — raising it shifts style, and
the yardstick punishes the shift. The 0.10 calibration sits at a
measured local optimum, and the ladder orders cleanly on the wide
slate (34 < 42 < 46 < 48). Strength verdict: no skill-dial move.

But cadence separated from strength:

- Mirror stalls at 620/c36: 4/24 on skirmish (the sweep's 14/48
  restricted to its seed range agrees exactly).
- 620/c30: worse on BOTH instruments — 32/48 yardstick (below Easy;
  it resurrects the 0.7 Standard-stall blemish, 3 unresolved vs
  Standard) and 6/24 mirror stalls. The cadence response is
  non-monotonic. Don't interpolate; measure every point.
- 620/c26: yardstick par at 2x sample (81 vs 82 of 96), mirror
  stalls 0/24, confirmed 2/48 at 48 games, decisions ~15% sooner
  (median 27.5k -> 23.4k).

An instrument lesson caught mid-campaign: Open Circuit Medium mirrors
read 24/24 undecided at the 40k cap in BOTH configurations — then
8/8 decided at a 100k cap, median ~50k. The large-map classes pace
past 40k by design (the 0.10 tens-of-minutes target); a 40k-cap
probe misreads pacing as stalling. Match the cap to the map class
before diagnosing.

## Verdict

**Medium ships at skill 620, cadence 26** (was 36). Gates:

- In-tree ladder tripwire: green (strict ordering + Expert sweep).
- Wide slate: Easy 34 < Medium 41 < Hard 46 < Expert 48.
- Entropy at Medium, 25 maps x 2 seeds: 2.22 bits over 160 seats;
  every map >= 1.8 except Scrapyard Brawl's 1.00, as always (the
  quick map ends before tech amortizes, by design).
- Final sweep at the shipped 0.12 configuration: 46 victories / 2
  undecided of 48 — against 33/15 at the 0.11 dials. Median decision
  23,206 ticks.
- Bless blast radius: `skirmish-t1200.png` only (the golden's seats
  take Medium from the embedded scenario); `state-hashes.json` pins
  Expert and did not move.

Still true and deliberately untouched: the artifact-specific Cupric
skirmish lean (4-42 at Medium here; the known-issues ledger carries
it) — a weights matter for the retraining campaign, not a dial. The
harvest starvation ladder (the "never ventured further" complaint)
is the next phase.

## The starvation ladder

The structural half of "the bot never ventured further": the economy
chore assigned only the nearest *known* node, refused anything deeper
in the enemy's half than its own, and unexplored scrap does not exist
to a fog-honest mind. A dry home patch was a dead economy unless the
policy happened to spend decision windows on Scout.

The ladder (`UtilityPolicy::prospect`, a GymBot chore behind the
normal economy channel — the scripted tiers never climb it, so the
yardstick they define stays byte-identical): rung 1 re-tries starved
harvesters with the enemy-side rule dropped (a dry home half makes
contested nodes acceptable); rung 2, nothing known at all, sends
exactly one prospector (lowest id) down a centre-then-corners sweep
so far scrap can enter `known_scrap`. Deliberately disjoint from the
scout machinery: prospecting never stamps `scouted_at` — that feeds
the trained `intel_age` feature, and a chore that writes a trained
feature decorrelates it from the action that produced it in training.
Both properties are pinned by `sim/tests/bot_prospect.rs` on a
starvation-box fixture (one-tile home patch; the only other scrap 14
Chebyshev out, enemy-side — reaching it takes the whole ladder).

Results, same instruments:

- Skirmish sweep: 48/48 decided (the campaign started at 33/15).
  Median decision fell 23.2k -> 5.7k ticks: renders show why —
  mid-map scrap and battlefield wrecks are now reachable, so the
  stronger seat's push arrives funded instead of stalling out. The
  playtest's "built max units with nearby scrap and then completely
  stalled" is exactly the repaired path.
- Oxide Flats (the sparse 101x51 map) at a 100k cap: 16/16 decided,
  median 17.5k.
- Entropy at Medium: 2.27 bits (floor 1.8; was 2.22 before the
  ladder).
- Wide ladder ordering re-measured post-change: 36 < 41 < 46 < 48 —
  intact with margin, Expert still sweeps; Easy firms up slightly
  (the ladder feeds the weakest rung's economy most).
- Bless blast radius: one line — `open-circuit` in
  state-hashes.json, the map whose 800-scrap home allotment (the
  smallest shipped) starves inside the 2,000-tick fixture window.

One environment note for the retraining campaign: the ladder lives in
the GymBot chore path, so training episodes now include it — the
policy will learn on a fed economy rather than learning to route
around starvation with Scout actions. That is the intended division
of labor between executive and policy.

## The jitter that wasn't (the settle probe)

The playtest reported "trying to get around each other jitters" and
the plan hypothesized a settled-crowd micro-oscillation (epsilon
overlaps plus the tick-parity alternation) to be fixed with a
collision deadband. The probe (`sim/tests/behavior_settle.rs`)
refutes the premise:

- **A parked crowd is EXACTLY stationary.** Ten harvesters
  group-moved into a cluster and left alone: 200/200 post-settle
  ticks with zero position change, to the fixed-point bit. Waypoint
  acceptance, arrival propagation, and the ring fan-out park crowds
  without residual overlap — there is nothing for a deadband to fix,
  and it would have shipped as dead weight with a hash bless.
  (The plan review's unverified finding had already flagged half of
  this: the parity flip reverses sweep ORDER, not push direction.)
- **A working crowd never stops moving, by design.** Eight
  harvesters on one node: 300/300 ticks in motion — the doorstep
  dance of extraction rotation, exactly the "crowds jostle but can't
  deadlock" contract. Not a defect.

What the playtest saw is transient mid-march jostling — two streams
crossing, each pushed off its path and re-seeking — which is
movement-feel tuning (waypoint acceptance vs push interplay), not a
collision-trigger bug, and none of the candidate collision dials
address it. Verdict: **no dial moves**; the stationarity property is
now pinned forever by the regression test, and the probe stays
in-tree as the instrument for any future movement-feel campaign.
