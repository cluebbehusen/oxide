# The 0.13 incumbent-continuation campaign

## Question

Can the decisive shipped v6 actor be recovered as a trainable parent, taught the
new repair verbs, and made visibly more varied without repeating the
from-scratch campaign's stalls and faction skew?

Artifact under test:

- Shipped ladder digest: `7e6f1303875b983e`
- Gym contract: v6, 65 raw features plus 3 conditioning inputs, 24 actions
- Architecture: three 384-wide tanh layers with a Q12 policy head

## Preflight findings

The failed campaign exposed training-process problems that must be removed before
another long run:

- The per-decision personality reward could total `+6.25` over a capped match,
  versus `+1` for a win. It rewarded keeping the game alive in the preferred
  posture more than ending it.
- `value_only=True` still updated the actor's shared trunk. A reconstructed
  critic could therefore damage the recovered actor during its supposed warmup.
- Warmup used the checkpoint's absolute update, so a resumed parent skipped it.
- Training paired Ferrous with even seats and Cupric with odd seats. It never
  crossed faction with physical seat, matching the rejected candidate's faction
  failure.
- `--maps grand` abandoned the small and standard maps used by the promotion
  slate. The long-map tech curriculum also discarded the incumbent's short-map
  finishing behavior.
- The fun gate checked cost-weighted mean composition, but not the reported
  per-seat floor or the share of matches decided. Cheap-unit body spam could
  look healthy when a few expensive specialists carried the value mix.

The Q12 policy is recoverable even though its critic and optimizer were not
exported. Direct dequantization by `coefficient / 4096` followed by export
reproduced every policy tensor exactly. A probe over 2,233 real decisions found
zero greedy-action disagreements between the recovered float actor and the
integer runtime.

## Balance hypothesis check

Before changing unit stats, the current matchup arena tested whether absent
Turrets were simply weak:

```text
scuttler:10 (400 scrap) vs turret:4 (400 scrap)
  turrets won both seat-swapped legs, retaining 200 scrap

sentinel:12 (1080 scrap) vs turret:6 (600 scrap), dense pitch
  turrets won both legs and eliminated every attacker, retaining 250 scrap mean
```

This does not measure the strategic opportunity cost of constructing defenses,
but it rejects the simple claim that Turrets are tactically underpowered. Their
low usage is treated as a policy/curriculum problem until an economy-level
counterfactual says otherwise.

## Campaign plan

1. Recover two float parents from the shipped Q12 artifact: one exact anchor and
   one trainable parent with RepairUnit and BuildRepairBay lifted from their
   unreachable output floors.
2. Warm the new zero critic without moving any actor parameter.
3. Continue from the incumbent under a fixed KL anchor, no per-step style
   reward, crossed faction/seat assignments, and a fixed/random/grand map mix.
4. Run a natural continuation first. Run repair seeding only as a controlled
   comparison, rewarding successful effects rather than action selection.
5. Select checkpoints by decisiveness, faction parity, body and value
   composition, repair success, and the existing ladder gates. Do not select by
   recency.

repair verb survived greedy quantized play. The later checkpoints show that the

override the whole gate battery.

## Effect-seeded continuations

The seeded runs exposed an important instrumentation error before they produced
a useful policy. Rewarding a sampled repair action pays intent, including
lowering failures and redundant choices. The gym worker now reports successful
effects instead:

- positive welded hp for `RepairUnit`
- a newly completed Repair Bay, Turret, or Array

Those counters are trainer-only telemetry, not policy observations, so the v6
artifact contract remains unchanged. The trainer also crosses faction with
physical seat, samples all four ordered faction pairs, preserves an exact
incumbent opponent, and can mix fixed, random, and grand maps in one run.

Early effect-seeded checkpoints proved that both repair mechanisms are
learnable, but failed whole-policy selection. Some healed broadly while losing
the rusher canary; others kept strength but collapsed the body-time diversity
floor. Structure seeding made Turret and Array completions reachable during
training, but did not make either head row a safe promotion lever. These were
useful mechanism checks, not shipping candidates.

The balanced consolidation narrowed the search around a repaired parent. Its
coarse update 1500 cleared strength and composition, but issued no field weld
and built Bays only for Cupric. Update 1430 healed all eight controlled cases
but narrowly missed body-time p10 and the Veteran cup. A dense rerun therefore
saved every update around that boundary rather than guessing between the coarse
checkpoints.

## Dense-parent strength, generalization, and pace

The first native Q12 cup used 150 seeds for each scripted tier and each ordered
faction pair. Before healed veterans could rejoin, update 1438 exactly
preserved the incumbent's outcome matrix:

- Scrapheap, Standard, and Veteran: 3,600 / 3,600
- Prime: 300 / 1,200, all from the Ferrous-west cell
- draws: 0 / 4,800

Once full-health re-entry became real gameplay, the unchanged actor fell to
3,786/4,800: Cupric-east lost 57/150 Veteran games in both FC and CC. That was
the correct signal to adapt the policy to the repaired lifecycle rather than
ship a post-training executive change it had never experienced.

Before the lifecycle correction, the float checkpoint went 120/120 against
the rusher and 120/120 against Veteran, with the inherited 60/120 Prime split.
A fresh 12-seed random-map check matched the incumbent's Prime and rusher
outcomes and finished faster. On the 2v2 team slate it improved the
scripted-tier total from 33/96 to 43/96.

That selection-stage actor's initial 12-map FFA sample favored the incumbent,

|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Update 1438 | 37 | 2 | 9,287 |

The paired map verdict was better on 8, worse on 13, and identical on 39;

## What the apparent stalls were

The 20,000-tick composition slate labels every still-running match as capped,
but that is not synonymous with economic exhaustion. Traces of the suspicious
matches usually retained live Harvesters, active production and combat, and
between 613 and 11,275 unharvested map scrap. With the exact affordability
guard, the 40,000-tick slates decided all 75 Medium matches and all 50 Expert
matches. The cap label was usually a long active war, not a dead economy.

One genuine repair/economy lock did exist. In the original Expert Gatework
Array seed 7000 trace, seat 6 issued 2,041 RepairUnit commands and stalled 1,932
times for insufficient scrap while the match capped; 46% of map scrap remained.
Preserving the sole Harvester was necessary but not sufficient because a
second Harvester could still keep retrying an unfunded weld. With the exact
opening-debit guard and healed-veteran lifecycle, the same match decides at
tick 17,818. Seat 6 issues 107 repairs with 17 affordability stalls, makes 109
deliveries for 1,046 scrap, trains 17 units, and lands 229 hits. It never
builds a Reclaimer, so the recovery came from restoring ordinary economy and
combat choices rather than injecting a resource source.

Repair lowering also preserves the last living economy worker. The Foundry's
separate recovery rule remains deliberately narrow: it trickles only when the
player has no living or queued Harvester and stops once a replacement is
affordable.

A Reclaimer costs 150 scrap, takes 350 ticks to build, and yields one scrap
every 30 ticks. Its simple payback is about 4,500 ticks after completion, so it
is plausible late insurance but a poor universal opening. The current v6
observation also lacks near-home known-scrap pressure, so it cannot cleanly
distinguish a safe Reclaimer investment from an abundant local field.
Unconditional income or a free starting Reclaimer would therefore change every
opening without addressing the dominant traced failure. The evidence supports
the targeted last-worker and repair-affordability guards now; a future v7
should add a fog-honest near-home scrap feature and successful
Reclaimer-completion telemetry before training that choice explicitly.

## Rejected output surgery

Small Q12 bias changes around update 1438 traded one gate for another:
increasing repair reach damaged scripted-tier strength, while decreasing it
reopened the capability hole. Transplanting the Array or Turret output rows
from structure-heavy checkpoints did not materially change their one-seed
usage. The exact checkpoint is a narrow whole-network solution; no head-row
quota or post-training surgery ships.
