# The 0.13 strategy and balance lab

## Question

Why does the promoted policy still omit Reclaimers, Arrays, and ordinary
Repair Bays, and rarely build Turrets? Which failures belong to the policy
contract and training method, and which structures fail to repay their real
whole-match opportunity cost?

Artifact under test:

- `inc013-rejoin-a` update 1450
- Q12 digest `fe2b97bdb7058372`
- float checkpoint
  `runs/inc013-rejoin-a/pool/ckpt-01450.pt` for exploratory action diagnostics

Raw match duration is not a negative metric. A long match with continuing
harvest, production, damage, repairs, territory changes, and credible attacks
is active play. A stagnant or cyclic state with no viable route to victory is
the failure.

## The saving bottleneck

The current mask exposes a build action only after the bank already covers its
price. In eight ordinary games the Reclaimer action was legal for only 16
decision windows, versus 176 for Turrets. The network cannot choose a persistent
goal such as "save 150 scrap for a Reclaimer." It must learn an unexplained
sequence of Idle choices while cheaper units remain legal, then win a very
short action window before another purchase spends the bank.

This makes the current macro interface hostile to long-horizon investments
independently of their balance.

The bottleneck is broader than construction. The 65-feature actor emits one
categorical choice, so production, construction, maintenance, scouting, and
army command all compete for the same winner. The scripted `UtilityPolicy`
already uses the more natural RTS shape: independent channels reconciled under
one shared budget.

An eight-episode behavior-cloning audit found 10,047 labels, of which 8,922
(88.8%) were Idle. The demonstrations contained no Scuttler, Bombard,
anti-air, Flak, Bastion, Array, AirRaid, Recall, Salvage, RepairUnit, or Repair
Bay actions. The two narrow teachers won only four of eight games against
Scrapheap. A larger MLP cannot infer strategic branches that are absent from
both the examples and the action interface.

## Dense-reward bias

The existing training potential is not composition-neutral:

```text
(ground_dps_strength + 25 * harvesters
 + standing_building_cost / 3 + ground_unit_wounds / 3) / 500
```

Banked scrap, queued units, and construction sites are worth zero. Dedicated
anti-air and air-superiority machines also contribute zero to
`ground_dps_strength`. A completed Scuttler earns about 0.50 potential units
per scrap after 80 training ticks, while a completed Turret earns 0.33 per
scrap after 300 builder ticks. Healing ground anti-air removes its wound claim
without adding ground DPS, so that weld can produce negative shaping.

This reward makes the observed Scuttler equilibrium easier to learn and
capital investment harder to learn even if the game itself is balanced. The
next contract will use owned material instead: bank, carried scrap, queues,
sites, and hp-weighted purchase value for every unit and building. Composition
and structure use remain evaluation descriptors, not usage rewards.

## Cost-paying whole-match interventions

Each intervention used the same update-1450 policy on both seats across all 16
shipped duel maps. The treated seat idled until it could pay for and issue one
specified build, then returned to the unmodified greedy policy. A separate
same-map, same-seed baseline classified winner flips. Structures paid their
normal price, consumed normal builder time, used normal placement lowering, and
could be destroyed normally.

Blind opening interventions:

| Forced opening | Positive winner flips | Negative winner flips | Unchanged |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Turret | 5 | 7 | 20 |
| Bastion | 5 | 12 | 15 |
| Array | 5 | 11 | 16 |
| Reclaimer | 9 | 6 | 17 |
| Repair Bay | 7 | 12 | 13 |

The arena result did not predict whole-game value. A Turret can win an
equal-cost fight and still be a losing opening after placement, builder time,
immobility, and foregone production are charged. Reclaimers were the only
opening intervention with a positive net point estimate.

Contextual interventions:

| Plan | Triggered | Completed | Positive flips | Negative flips | Unchanged |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Array after Fabricator | 30 | 30 | 9 | 8 | 15 |
| Repair Bay after 100 wound-value | 28 | 17 | 1 | 1 | 30 |
| Reclaimer after tick 4,000 | 32 | 20 | 4 | 1 | 27 |
| Bastion after visible contact | 32 | 19 | 4 | 7 | 21 |
| Turret after a Harvester loss | 19 | 1 | 1 | 0 | 31 |

Context changes the answer. Arrays move from a clearly harmful blind opening to
roughly neutral after tech. Wound-triggered Bays are neutral in this sample.
Late Reclaimers have a favorable point estimate. The reactive Turret is usually
selected too late to finish, which implicates threat observability, construction
time, and placement as well as weapon stats.

## Interim Reclaimer verdict

The promoted policy builds zero Reclaimers in ordinary Medium and Expert
composition slates. The current building costs 150 scrap, occupies a Harvester
for 350 build ticks, and returns one scrap per 30 ticks: about 4,500 operating
ticks to repay the price.

That payback is slow, but the intervention result does not support "strictly
underpowered." A forced early Reclaimer flipped nine baselines toward its owner
and six away; a tick-4,000 plan flipped four toward and one away. The stronger
diagnosis is that the action contract provides no way to save for it and the
observation omits known remaining node value near home. Price and income still
need variant tests because a viable building can remain too narrow to appear in
normal play.

A larger paired study prebuilt a full-price Reclaimer for each treated seat
across all 16 duel maps, eight seeds, and both seats: 256 paired comparisons.
At the current 150 cost, 350-tick build time, and 30-tick income period, its
score change was +0.010 with clustered 95% CI [-0.055, +0.074]. It was strongly
bad on quick maps (-0.375) and mildly positive on standard and large maps
(+0.080 and +0.070). Median lifetime was 3,321 ticks and mean gross yield only
113 scrap, below its sticker price.

A free Reclaimer scored +0.201, but compared with an equal 150-scrap cash gift
the building added only +0.055 and the interval crossed zero. This separates
the value of the asset from the value of simply subsidizing a player.

Changing income from one scrap per 30 ticks to one per 20 improved the paid
point estimate to +0.037 and the free-versus-cash estimate to +0.111, but the
direct period comparison was not conclusive and introduced one genuine
no-progress Open Circuit cap. Cutting cost to 100 still produced zero natural
Reclaimers. Cost 75 finally made 8.8% of seats build them, sometimes four per
seat because the neural mask has no cap, and added a capped game. Large price
cuts therefore act as a brittle policy switch rather than fixing strategic
reasoning.

The working verdict is observation and reservation first. A modest income
improvement remains a candidate only if the new controller still finds the
current economics too narrow.

A follow-up at one scrap per 24 ticks kept the full-price paid estimate near
neutral (+0.020, clustered 95% CI [-0.074, +0.113]) while the free building
beat an equal 150-scrap gift by +0.129, CI [+0.023, +0.234]. Its one capped
normal match had moved only 27 ticks before the limit and was still active.
Unlike the period-20 treatment, it produced no observed resource deadlock.
The first v7 physics therefore uses period 24, leaving cost 150, build time
350, and the cap of two unchanged. The purpose is a three-minute insurance
payback, not shorter matches.

## Whole-match defense and support variants

Paired full-match gifts tested each structure at normal price rather than
inferring value from an arena. A second treatment disabled Salvage so the
structure's combat or support effect could be separated from the actor's
liquidation policy.

The first-v7 verdict is to leave Turret, Array, and Repair Bay stats unchanged.
Across the gift studies, the current actor salvaged 86-89% of the structures.
Price changes mostly moved actions across affordability-mask cliffs and caused
spam rather than demonstrating better strategic value.

### Turret

A paid/prebuilt Turret changed score by -0.013, clustered 95% CI
[-0.081, +0.055]. Keeping it unsalvageable changed score by -0.023, CI
[-0.102, +0.055]. Its value was contextual: an equal-value retained Turret was
+0.375 on quick maps, roughly neutral on standard maps, and -0.094 on large
maps. Gifted Turrets fired only 1.5 times on average and 170 of 192 were sold.

Cost 90 raised natural reach from 8.8% to 20%. Cost 80 made 87.5% of seats
build, averaging 3.54 Turrets per building seat because the old neural mask had
no cap. Alongside the healthy equal-cost arena counters, this argues for a cap
of two and earlier/contextual construction, not more range or damage.

### Array

A retained full-price Array changed score by -0.129, CI [-0.207, -0.051],
despite producing 1,265 radar-contact ticks on average. Cost 90 was worse
(-0.168, CI [-0.246, -0.090]) and reached only 1.2% naturally. An aggressive
cost-60 plus effect buff made 66.2% of seats build, averaging 2.91 Arrays per
building seat.

The Array is not paying for itself under the current actor, but cheaper
information does not help an actor that cannot turn blips into a response. The
first v7 run keeps cost 120, caps it at one, exposes home pressure, and trains
operational reactions. Price is reconsidered only after that controller can use
the information.

### Repair Bay

A retained 200-value Bay lost 0.160 score versus 200 liquid scrap, CI
[-0.266, -0.055], and added no healing over the cash treatment because the cash
funded field welds. Cost 150 remained negative (-0.125, CI [-0.219, -0.031]).
A cost-100 Bay with stronger radius and pulse made 92.5% of seats build one and
reduced composition diversity without improving 20,000-tick decisions.

The favorable wounded-unit fixture still proves the Bay works. The first v7
run therefore conditions it on real wound value and caps it at one. Placement
and rear-rally coverage are better next levers than a universal capital
discount.

## What the 20,000-tick caps actually are

The promoted Medium slate capped on Basalt Spine, Gatework Array, and Slag
Expanse. Their last meaningful progress at tick 20,000 was only 185, 8, and 47
ticks old. They retained 2,596, 11,487, and 2,500 scrap on the map, had
delivered 22,800 scrap in total, and landed 4,996 hits. Extending the identical
runs resolved all three at ticks 23,423, 23,174, and 21,708.

These are active long wars, not resource exhaustion. Match duration is not a
failure metric; the gate must distinguish continuing combat and economy from a
deadlock or cyclic policy.

There is still a genuine policy loop. Of 4,159 Push choices, 2,519 (60.6%)
were issued while an army was already Pushing or Engaging. In capped games the
ratio was 64.0%. The orders were productive, but redundant re-commitment
dominated the strategic action stream and made the long wars less varied. Push
should represent a state transition, not a button the actor can spam in flight.

## Natural strategic reach

Across 25 shipped maps, 80 seats, and 36,428 Medium decisions:

- 78 seats completed a Fabricator, with median completion at tick 1,145.
- The bank reached 120 scrap in 352 windows (0.97%), 150 in 114 (0.31%), and
  never reached 200.
- Turret converted 1,028 legal windows into 22 selections; Array converted
  352 into zero; Reclaimer converted 114 into zero; Repair Bay was never legal.
- The 20 completed Turrets belonged to seven seats, all Ferrous on team maps.
  Only three of those seats recorded any Turret fire.
- Array was the unmasked top logit 4,137 times, always while unaffordable. Once
  legal it trailed the winner by 13.15 logit units on average.

The actor often wants a structure at the wrong economic moment, then forgets
it before the bank arrives. This is direct evidence for an explicit persistent
plan and reserved budget rather than a broad structure buff.

Faction conditioning is also uneven. In equal decision counts, Ferrous chose
no anti-air unit and all 22 Turret attempts; Cupric chose 234 Stingers and no
Turrets. Both factions saw essentially identical Turret and Array legality.
Several advanced roster branches remain dead independently of matchup balance.

Personality conditioning changes the aggressive extreme but does not create a
defensive personality. In a controlled 48-match set, attack share among
non-idle actions was 9.0%, 9.6%, and 22.9% at aggression 250, 500, and 900.
None of the three personalities built a tactical structure. The low setting is
a less aggressive tactician, not a fortifier.

## Controller experiment selected

The next prototype keeps deterministic feed-forward Q12 inference but replaces
the single winner with three independent heads over one trunk:

1. production,
2. construction and maintenance,
3. military operations.

Construction goals become legal before affordability. A selected goal persists,
reserves its cost, and is executed when labor and funds arrive. Redundant
structures receive explicit committed caps. Push is legal only as a real army
state transition. New fog-honest features expose salvage remaining near home,
queues, construction sites, free labor, hp-weighted assets, home pressure, and
the current capital plan.

The continuous personality input remains for compatibility, but it is joined by
a deterministic four-way match strategy code: fortify, industry, combined
arms, or pressure. The first test will widen the update-1450 actor into the
factorized contract, then compare the untrained bridge, behavior-distilled
initialization, and anchored PPO continuation. A recurrent policy is deferred:
memory cannot repair an action that is masked until affordable, and recurrence
would add deployment and replay complexity before the more direct interface
failure is tested.

## Untrained factorized bridge

The exact promoted Q12 artifact was widened without changing any inherited
coefficient. Sixteen feature columns and four strategy columns entered at zero.
The construction and operations no-op rows copied the old Idle row. Digest:
`a3de367bb0763816`.

This deliberately non-behavior-preserving bridge isolated what factorization
alone changes. On the shipped 25-map Medium slate, one seed per map, it decided
15 of 25 by tick 20,000. Every cap remained active: the oldest last-progress
age was 259 ticks. Army value entropy was 2.27 bits and per-seat p10 was 1.44,
but the actor built almost every structure to its cap:

- Fabricator 1.00, Array 0.82, Bastion 1.60, Flak 1.23, Reclaimer 1.99,
  Repair Bay 1.00, and Turret 1.75 completed per seat.
- All seats completed a Fabricator, Reclaimer, and Repair Bay.
- Scuttler body-time fell from 29.3% in the v6 parent to 0.1%.

That is reach without judgment. The same bridge won only 10/20 against
Scrapheap and 0/20 against each of Standard, Veteran, and Prime in a quick cup.
It did heal all eight repair fixtures, completing eight Bays and issuing 54
field-weld commands. The interface unlocks the verbs, but the inherited
single-action logits do not compose sensibly when every head fires at once.
Behavior distillation is required before PPO.

## Behavior-distillation curriculum

The first four-strategy teacher was itself a failed experiment. It reserved a
Fabricator, then Array, then Bastion before funding a defensive unit. All 64
episodes lost to Scrapheap; 29,664 of 37,856 duplicated production labels were
Harvester intentions blocked behind capital. This was not an optimizer failure.
The demonstrations encoded an economically losing priority.

The corrected curriculum:

- establishes workers, cheap Sentinels, and a small fighting screen before
  optional infrastructure;
- waits for a live construction site to finish before commissioning another,
  so completed-kind targets do not accidentally create two walking claims;
- gives quick-map Turrets priority over luxury capital;
- stages one Reclaimer instead of immediately reserving two;
- varies army composition and commitment thresholds across fortify, industry,
  combined-arms, and pressure profiles.

With those constraints, all four teachers won from both seats against
Scrapheap in the deterministic smoke pair. The earlier 64-episode revision,
before the construction-site wait was added, won 48/64 and produced a Q12
candidate with digest `f32275e49ce9405c`. That candidate was useful but
refused: it reached 17/25 decisions at tick 20,000, 2.64-bit army-value
entropy, per-seat p10 1.53, Arrays on 90% of seats, Reclaimers on 77.5%, and
Bays on 22.5%, but the balanced profile inherited its losing combined-arms
demonstrations. In the quick cup it won only from seat 0, scoring 10/20 against
Scrapheap, Standard, and Veteran and 0/20 against Prime. Strategic reach does
not excuse a faction/seat collapse.

The full corpus used 256 episodes across every shipped duel map, both learner
seats, all four ordered faction pairs, all four strategies, and Scrapheap,
Standard, and Veteran teachers. It produced 427,964 labeled decisions. Every
unit, structure, repair verb, and operation appeared in the labels. The
teachers won 115/256 games, distributed as 39 fortify, 52 industry, 16
combined-arms, and 8 pressure wins.

## Windowed PPO control

The first no-bonus control resumed epoch 15 for 250 updates with 384-decision
windows, a fixed 0.10 KL anchor, 25 critic-only updates, all four faction
pairs, mixed map scales, self-play, history, scripted tiers, the rush teacher,
FFA, and the widened incumbent. It preserved the new behavior and improved
the cup:

- updates 150, 200, and 250 swept Scrapheap, Standard, and Veteran from both
  seats;
- all three still won only one seat against Prime and lost both seats to the
  rush teacher;
- update 250 Q12 digest `e78861a6336039b1` healed all eight repair fixtures;
- its 40,000-tick shipped-map slate decided 21/25, with only the four large
  multiplayer maps capped; their last-progress ages were 59-599 ticks;
- across 80 seats it reached Turrets on 67.5%, Arrays on 88.8%, and Reclaimers
  on 30.0%, with value-entropy p10 1.85 and body-time dominance p90 52.1%.

This control is refused. It demonstrates that the factorized interface solves
strategic reach without unit spam, but not that the original PPO loop can turn
that reach into robust strength. The training entropy stayed only 0.03-0.08
nats per head. The in-loop score plateaued at 1.0 against Veteran, 0.5 against
Prime, and 0.0 against the rush teacher.

The control also exposed three measurement errors in the training setup:

1. A 384-decision batch covers only 6,144 game ticks, while an episode may run
   40,000. The policy changes several times before one game's terminal result
   arrives. With GAE lambda 0.95, one percent of that result remains after
   about 89 decisions, only 1,424 ticks.
2. The so-called incumbent lane applies three-head arbitration to widened v6
   logits. It therefore plays the weak overbuilding bridge, not the decisive
   single-action parent.
3. Opponent weights count worker processes rather than learner rows. A
   two-sided self-play worker contributes twice the PPO rows of a tier or FFA
   worker. The nominal 25% self share in this run was actually 40%.

The next comparison uses one complete episode per frozen-policy collection,
time-limit value bootstrapping, GAE lambda as an explicit experiment dial, a
legacy one-winner incumbent adapter, and learner-row-weighted role allocation.
No structure, repair, diversity, or game-length reward is used in the control.

## Complete-episode PPO correction

The windowed control did not provide a clean experiment on long-horizon
strategy. Its policy could change several times during one match, while the
eventual terminal result had almost no influence on decisions near the
opening. The corrected collector uses one complete episode per job, freezes
the acting policy for that episode, and permits at most 2,500 policy decisions.
Variable-length episodes are padded only for batching, and padded rows are
excluded from PPO updates. A living seat at the time limit is marked
`truncated`, receives zero terminal reward, and bootstraps from its final value;
an eliminated seat remains terminal with reward -1.

The corrected setup also:

- makes GAE lambda and entropy coefficient explicit; the selected continuation
  used lambda 1.0 and entropy coefficient 0.002;
- allocates opponent roles by expected learner rows rather than worker count,
  accounting for the two learner lanes produced by self-play and team play;
- uses a separate seeded RNG for PPO minibatch shuffling, so evaluation and
  probe calls cannot perturb training order;
- assigns distinct Scrapheap, Standard, Veteran, and Prime scripted roles
  instead of sampling an opaque generic tier; and
- preserves Torch's global RNG across in-loop Q12 probes.

The attempted exact-v6 opponent adapter was removed. Applying a one-winner v6
policy through v7 masks and lowering did not reproduce the decisive v6
opponent; it was another weak transformed policy, not a legitimate incumbent
anchor. This matters because an inaccurately named opponent can make a league
look stronger without adding any real pressure.

## Competitive-survivor gate

A capped match is not automatically a draw in the behavioral sense. The
promotion battery now keeps two separate quantities:

1. wins, which measure the ability to finish; and
2. competitive survivors, defined as wins plus caps that still show recent
   combat, economy, production, or roster movement.

An inactive cap does not count, and a loss never counts. Active caps also do
not become wins in the reported strength score. This prevents two opposite
measurement errors: rejecting a bot for sustaining a long competitive war,
or accepting a passive turtle because it remained alive until an arbitrary
limit.

The distinction is material. Update 80's Standard cell is 76 wins plus four
active caps, so it has 80/80 competitive survivors but only 76/80 finishes.
Update 75's rush cell is ten wins plus ten active caps, not a misleading 50%
strength result and not a fabricated sweep. By contrast, update 80's 20 Prime
losses are failures even though its aggregate cup is strong.

No training reward pays the bot to shorten a match. Game duration remains a
descriptor. The desired pressure is to avoid inactive states and losses, not
to make every viable long strategy rush the clock.

## Pressure-only continuation rejected

A second continuation started from update 80 and concentrated 58.8% of

Direct output-bias edits, checkpoint interpolation, and model-soup trials
reached the same qualitative dead end: they moved failures between gates
instead of improving the whole policy. At this stage, the selected artifact
remained the unmodified update 80.

## Personality cliff and the dealt distribution

The four-way strategy code is a hard aggression quartile:

- 0-249: fortify;
- 250-499: industry;
- 500-749: combined arms;
- 750-1000: pressure.

This produces useful strategic separation, but it also creates a discontinuity
at 750 that the continuous aggression scalar does not smooth away. In generated
duels, aggression 750 decided 12/12 games and had strong body-time diversity
(2.323 bits, p10 1.814). Its native cup was much worse: it won Veteran and
rusher only in the Ferrous-favored seat/roster cell and lost 0/20 to Prime.
A profile can look lively in self-play and still fail as an opponent.

The industry and combined-arms profiles provide genuinely different play. In
the generated one-seed audit, aggression 250 built Reclaimers on 79.2% of
seats; aggression 500 built none, but built Turrets and Arrays on nearly every
seat. The pressure profile added air-heavy variation but was not robust enough
to deal to ordinary matches.

The former uniform 250-900 deal crossed both the hard 750 boundary and an
inactive tail below it. The first measured tail appeared at aggression 625.
The final deal therefore selects industry three times in five and combined
arms twice in five from two measured-safe bands:

- industry, uniformly 250-399; or
- combined arms, uniformly 500-600.

The unmeasured 400-499 transition and every profile above 600 are skipped.
Explicit custom personalities retain the full 0-1000 range for experiments.
This is a containment decision, not evidence that the pressure strategy has
been trained successfully.

## Reclaimer reach is not Reclaimer availability

The current Reclaimer costs 150 scrap, takes 350 builder ticks, and produces
one scrap every 24 ticks. Its sticker-price repayment is 3,600 operating ticks,
or about three minutes at 20 Hz. Natural construction is no longer absent:

| Cohort | Seats that built a Reclaimer |
|---|---:|
| Update 75, shipped Medium, two seeds | 17.5% |
| Update 75, shipped Expert, one seed | 33.8% |
| Generated default duels | 22.9% |
| Generated industry duels at aggression 250 | 79.2% |

The more important late-game audit is survival. Final snapshots of capped
cohorts retained Reclaimers on only 1/16 shipped cap seats, 0/6 generated-duel
cap seats, 0/4 generated 2v2 cap seats, and 0/20 generated FFA cap seats. In
the one capped aggression-250 duel, both players had built three Reclaimers
over the match; all six were destroyed before tick 40,000.

The bot therefore does build Reclaimers, and the industry personality builds
them early and often. Cost and income are not the whole problem. Placement,
defense, survival to the 3,600-tick repayment point, and rebuilding after a
loss determine whether the structure functions as long-war insurance. A
destroyed historical Reclaimer cannot prevent a late resource lock.

The period-24 improvement is retained because its paid intervention remained
near neutral while a free building beat an equal cash gift, and because the
new controller uses it naturally without a price cliff. Further broad cost
cuts are rejected. The next Reclaimer-specific training experiment should
measure protected lifetime and rebuild latency, not reward raw build count.

## A real resource deadlock

Most capped matches in this campaign were active, but one personality-sweep
case establishes that a genuine resource lock is possible:

- update 75, aggression 625, Rustbelt Canyon;
- capped at tick 40,000;
- last combat 17,859 ticks old;
- last economy activity 18,112 ticks old;
- last roster change 17,939 ticks old;
- six scrap remained on the entire map;
- the two banks held two and six scrap;
- each player retained one Harvester, with no queued Harvester and no
  completed Reclaimer.

This is categorically different from a cap with a hit, delivery, unit, or
movement in the last few hundred ticks. Neither side had a plausible economic
route out of the state.

An unconditional one-scrap-per-60-ticks Foundry income from tick zero was
rejected. On the default three-seed update-75 slate it reduced decisions from
69/75 to 66/75 and increased caps from six to nine. It also materially changed
the native opening matchup: the previously strong Prime cell fell to 0/20.
Free opening income is not a harmless deadlock patch.

The selected rule is a late-only floor:

- no baseline credit before tick 12,000;
- after that point, one scrap per 60 ticks for each non-resigned player with a
  living completed Foundry;
- credit is per player, not per Foundry;
- the existing no-Harvester recovery remains faster at one scrap per ten ticks
  until a replacement Harvester is affordable.

Replaying the aggression-625 Rustbelt case with the late floor produced a
decision at tick 20,047 instead of the deadlocked 40,000-tick cap. The floor
does not exist to make games short. It preserves the opening and normal
midgame, then guarantees that a living Foundry eventually provides another
strategic choice after nodes and Reclaimers are gone. A Reclaimer remains much
better insurance: it can operate before tick 12,000 and produces 2.5 times as
fast.

## Interim campaign verdict

The decisive model change was the policy contract, not a larger network:
independent production, construction, and operations decisions; persistent
capital reservation; committed structure caps; and fog-honest economic,
pressure, queue, wound, and plan features. The untrained bridge proved that
the contract creates reach but not judgment. Broad behavior distillation made
the branches coherent, and short PPO continuation recovered competitive
strength. A recurrent model was not needed to solve an action that the old
mask made impossible to plan.

Update 80 was the selected v7 finalist at this stage. It uses the appended observations,
builds the previously missing structures, executes field repair, retains broad
unit composition, and survives or wins every lower-tier, Standard, Veteran,
and rush cup cell in the all-faction battery. Its known defect is the Ferrous
seat-1 Prime matchup. The failed pressure continuation shows that this should
not be "fixed" by blindly training longer on harder opponents.

The balance conclusions are narrower:

- keep Turret, Array, and Repair Bay combat/economic stats unchanged until the
  new controller's contextual use can be evaluated in shipped play;
- keep the Reclaimer at cost 150 and build time 350, with the tested period-24
  income;
- retain the emergency Harvester recovery; and
- add only the tick-12,000 Foundry floor as protection against demonstrated
  terminal resource locks.

Long active games are acceptable. The remaining work is to improve the
pressure strategy, Reclaimer survivability/rebuilding, and the Ferrous
Prime-seat defect without sacrificing the industry and combined-arms styles
that made the bot more varied.

## Final audit: the gate had been grading the wrong population

The first v7 selection tables were useful diagnostics, but they still mixed
post-defeat autonomous remnants and Harvesters into the diversity verdict.
That can make a losing army disappear from the sample or make one combat kind
look diverse because the economy supplied a second body type. Schema 6 fixes
the population:

- every seat contributes its entire competitive lifetime until resignation or
  loss of its completed Foundry;
- combat composition excludes non-weapon units;
- structures count only while the seat remains competitive;
- the raw per-seat arrays, not entropy of an averaged roster, drive the tail
  limits;
- p25 replaces small-sample p10 for the promotion threshold;
- dealt, fixed industry 300, and fixed combined-arms 550 profiles must all
  pass; and
- an active cap is not a duration failure. Only inactivity and resource
  exhaustion enter the cap-health gate.

The minimum promotion sample is three seeds per shipped map. The final
thresholds are 2.00/1.95 mean value/body entropy, 1.35/1.25 p25 entropy,
7.5% maximum catastrophic value/body tails, 10% maximum body-dominance tail,
50% maximum leading mean body share, and a 10% maximum unhealthy-cap rate.
The dealt profile also owes 90% Fabricator, 40% Turret, 60% Array, and 25%
Reclaimer reach. A paired parent can additionally limit regression in entropy,
p25, tails, and leading share.

This stricter gate changed the meaning of "fun." It does not reward building a
particular unit, punish long matches, or hide defeated armies. It asks whether
the bot repeatedly reaches and uses a varied strategy while it is still a
participant in the game.

Update 95 remained a strong v7 parent, but a short combat-mix continuation

  `runs/v7-u95-combatmix005-r1/probe/weights-00105.json`;
- digest `ca3502efb0a4e845`;
- 560 wins in the 800-game native all-faction, two-style cup; and

Two broad attempts to add the rare production branches were rejected.

The first behavior-cloning run reported infinite loss. `NoOperation` had been

## Selective supervised revival

The narrower hypothesis was that the parent already knew how to play the game
and only two production logits had fallen below the greedy frontier.
`revive.py` collected 55,877 corrected-teacher states from 64 deterministic
Scrapheap episodes, seeds 42,000 through 42,063, crossing duel maps, seats, and
factions. Train and held rows split by whole episode. Only production rows 3
and 8 could move; non-target teacher states distilled the parent's logits.

The exact release run is:

```sh
uv run revive.py \
  --initialize-from runs/v7-u95-combatmix005-r1/probe/weights-00105.json \
  --actions 3,8 --promote-actions 3 \
  --episodes 64 --tiers scrapheap --episode-seed-base 42000 \
  --max-ticks 40000 --cadence 16 \
  --steps 201 --lr 0.003 \
  --positive-batch 1024 --retention-batch 4096 \
  --retention-coef 0.01 --parameter-coef 0.0001 \
  --heldout-modulo 5 --sample-seed 91 \
  --out runs/release-row3.pt
uv run export.py --ckpt runs/release-row3.pt \
  --out runs/release-row3.json
```

The joint held audit found 91.97% Scuttler and 98.45% air-superiority greedy
accuracy. Only 61 of 15,805 held non-target rows newly chose either rare
action, a 0.386% rate, and mean KL was 0.00651. The saved-model audit then
restored row 8 and judged what actually ships: 91.97% held Scuttler reach,
15 of 15,934 non-target rows newly choosing it (0.094%), and mean KL 0.00366.
The trainer now guarantees that every selected class appears in each sampled
positive batch; an absent class fails loudly instead of producing an
empty-mean NaN.

`--promote-actions` is important. It trains the rows together, then restores
any row that failed downstream gates from the exact parent snapshot. The
release checkpoint therefore reproduces the jointly trained row 3 while
restoring row 8 exactly. The first collector mistakenly conditioned every
teacher strategy at skill 1000, even though shipped industry uses 620. That
artifact was discarded before release. The corrected collector shares the
runtime aggression-to-skill rule; its row differs, but all 800 native cup
outcomes and every reported median tick are identical to the discarded
artifact. The corrected native semantic digest is `4473f3e795891915`.

## Training setup audit

The model topology did not need to become recurrent or larger, but the training
setup required several structural corrections:

- complete episodes stay aligned in a rollout; truncation bootstraps a living
  learner while true terminals remain absorbing;
- the KL anchor anneals on the current phase's relative clock, not the imported
  checkpoint's absolute update. The phase's first optimizer update receives
  the requested coefficient; decay begins on the second;
- the named ladder no longer assumes the learned skill input is monotone.
  Industry conditions at 620 and combined arms at 1000 on every level, crossed
  during training with the exact Easy/Medium/Hard/Expert cadence and hesitation
  profiles;
- factorial, duel, and yardstick diagnostics use that same named wrapper by
  default. Only an explicit skill override selects a raw profile;
- every sampled cadence reaches the gym worker reset, and hesitation is passed
  explicitly instead of being re-derived from policy skill;
- the torch-side tournament now separates actor conditioning from execution,
  defaulting to combined 550 with Expert's zero hesitation and 28-tick cadence.
  Its old call passed skill 1000 as 1000-per-mille hesitation and therefore
  idled every default evaluation window; the native Q12 cup and release
  verdict were unaffected;
- every league phase claims a fresh write-once run directory before workers
  launch. A reused log, pool, probe, checkpoint, or unknown file refuses the
  run instead of joining a new phase to stale opponents;
- pool, evaluation, and probe schedules use the current phase's clock, and the
  phase always writes its final pool and latest checkpoints. A short
  continuation can no longer train successfully and leave only the imported
  parent on disk;
- opponent mixtures are normalized into a canonical order before deterministic
  job and RNG assignment, so spelling the same weighted mix in a different CLI
  order does not silently create a different campaign;
- checkpoint metadata records critic readiness. Behavior-cloning and exact-Q12
  recovery have untrained critics, selective revival preserves that state, and
  PPO warms an unready critic for 15 value-only updates before moving the
  actor. A ready league critic skips the redundant warm-up unless explicitly
  overridden;
- successful salvage and repair rewards come from observed simulation effects,
  not sampled intent. Field-weld telemetry comes from accepted HP tagged by
  the resolver, so a patient inside a Repair Bay cannot steal or fabricate
  weld credit;
- checkpoints and exports now carry a validated content-addressed lineage.
  The release lineage id is
  `sha256:41de14c644a34fa26597a717cc6f01883529b24325436651120682d55799fe70`.
  Paths are not identity, changed input bytes change the id, and tampered
  manifests refuse in both Python and the native Q12 gate. League, BC, and
  revival ancestry identifies the trainer, model, gym client, exact driver
  binary, and every consumed world source: fixed scenario contents, or the
  generator plus its locked Python environment. Generated-map caches are
  namespaced by the validator binary and verify their deterministic retry
  candidate before reuse, so stale or corrupt output is regenerated. Exact Q12
  recovery preserves its source lineage; action unflooring and contract
  widening derive new lineages. Replaying the final 64-episode revival under
  these hardened identities reproduced every policy tensor exactly; only the
  lineage changed from the pre-audit export.

## Final campaign verdict

The selected 0.13 bot is the update-105 factorized parent with only the jointly
trained Scuttler row promoted. It uses the new observations and three-head
contract, saves for capital projects, builds and fires Turrets, reaches Arrays,
builds Reclaimers early in its industry profile, and performs successful field
welds. It clears the stricter competitive-lifetime diversity gate, improves the
native rusher result, preserves every non-rusher cup cell, and introduces no
inactive or resource-exhausted cap in the final slate.

The selected balance remains conservative: Reclaimer cost 150, build time 350,
and period-24 income; no combat-stat changes to Turret, Array, or Repair Bay;
fast no-Harvester recovery; and the tick-12,000 Foundry floor for demonstrated
terminal resource locks. Match duration is deliberately neutral.
